AP Human Geography · Unit 3: Culture

Cultural patterns & processes (topics 3.1–3.8). Plan: notes 10 min → quiz alone 10 min → one FRQ 15 min → watch a review video for anything you missed.

1. The big ideas

  • Culture = shared beliefs, practices, traits of a group. Traits: language, religion, food, clothing, architecture. Norms = expected behavior; taboos = forbidden behavior.
  • Ethnocentrism = judging other cultures by your own. Cultural relativism = judging a culture by its own standards.
  • Folk culture: small, rural, traditional, changes slowly, spreads by relocation. Popular culture: large, urban, changes fast, spreads hierarchically through media → can cause placelessness (places look the same).
  • Cultural landscape = how people shape the land: buildings, land use, religious structures, signs, toponyms (place names). Sequent occupance = layers left by different groups over time (e.g., a city with Indigenous, Spanish and modern layers). Placemaking = shaping public space to reflect a community's identity.
  • Ethnic enclaves / neighborhoods (Chinatown, Little Italy) show culture in the landscape. Gender roles also shape space (who works where, who uses which spaces).

2. Diffusion: how culture spreads

TypeMeaningExample
RelocationPeople move and bring culture with themImmigrants open restaurants; Christianity carried by colonists
Expansion: contagiousSpreads person to person, like a virus, to nearby peopleA meme or slang spreading through a school
Expansion: hierarchicalSpreads from powerful/large places or people downFashion: Paris/NYC → big cities → small towns; a king converts, then the people
Reverse hierarchicalStarts small/rural, moves upWalmart (started rural); hip-hop (from neighborhoods to global)
StimulusThe idea spreads but gets changed to fit the local cultureMcDonald's McAloo Tikki (no beef) in India
Why diffusion happens:
Historical: colonialism, imperialism, trade routes (Silk Road), missionaries → spread of English, Spanish, French, Christianity, Islam.
Today: globalization, internet/social media, time-space compression (places feel closer because communication and travel are fast), urbanization, migration.

3. Religion & language

ReligionTypeHearthHow it spread
ChristianityUniversalizingLevant (Jerusalem/Bethlehem)Missionaries, Roman Empire (hierarchical), colonialism (relocation)

Universalizing = seeks converts, spreads widely. Ethnic = tied to one group and place, spreads mostly by relocation.

  • Language family → branch → group: English = Indo-European → Germanic → West Germanic. Indo-European is the largest family by speakers.
  • Lingua franca = common language for trade/communication between groups (English today; Swahili in East Africa).
  • Pidgin = simple mixed language for contact; creole = a pidgin that became people's first language (Haitian Creole).
  • Dialect = regional version of a language (accent, words, grammar).

6. FRQ practice (AP style)

How FRQs work: parts A, B, C… each worth 1 point. Read the verb!
  • Identify = name it (a few words).
  • Define = give the meaning.
  • Describe = give details/characteristics (1–2 sentences).
  • Explain = say how or why: use "because…", "this leads to…" (2–3 sentences).
  • Compare = one similarity or difference, clearly stated.
Always use the course term and a specific example.

Practice prompt: Popular culture spreads quickly around the world today.

A. Identify one type of expansion diffusion.

Check: contagious, hierarchical, or stimulus (reverse hierarchical also counts as hierarchical).

B. Describe how hierarchical diffusion spreads a fashion trend.

Check: starts in a large/influential place or with celebrities (NYC, Paris), then moves to other large cities, then to smaller towns — skips over nearby rural places.

C. Explain how time-space compression speeds up the diffusion of popular culture.

Check: the internet/social media/fast travel shrink the time it takes for ideas to move, so a song or trend can reach the whole world in days instead of years, regardless of distance.

D. Explain one way the spread of popular culture can threaten folk culture.

Check: people adopt global brands/media, so local traditions (crafts, dress, language, food) lose users → assimilation or loss of local identity → placelessness.

E. Explain how stimulus diffusion can protect local culture.

Check: the global idea is changed to fit local values (e.g., no beef menu items in India), so the local norm/taboo is kept while the new product spreads.

F. Compare a universalizing religion and an ethnic religion in how they diffuse.

Check: universalizing (Christianity/Islam/Buddhism) seeks converts → expansion + relocation across many regions; ethnic (Hinduism/Judaism) doesn't seek converts → mostly relocation with migrants, stays concentrated near its hearth.

G. Explain one way a religion can be seen in the cultural landscape.

Check: sacred buildings (mosques with minarets, churches with steeples, Hindu temples), cemeteries, pilgrimage sites (Mecca, the Ganges), place names (San Antonio, St. Louis) — and say what it shows about the people there.
