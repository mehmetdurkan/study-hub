Protein synthesis & mutations

Big question: How does an error during DNA replication affect protein synthesis and function? Read the notes (10 min), take the quiz alone without looking (10 min), then watch the links for anything you missed.

1. The path: DNA → RNA → protein

  • Replication (S phase, nucleus): DNA copies itself. DNA polymerase proofreads, but a few errors slip through. An error that stays = a mutation.
  • Transcription (nucleus): the gene's DNA is copied into mRNA. Base pairing: A→U, T→A, C→G, G→C. Example: template DNA TAC → mRNA AUG.
  • Translation (ribosome, cytoplasm): the ribosome reads mRNA 3 bases at a time (codons). Each tRNA has an anticodon that matches a codon and carries one amino acid. mRNA AUG ↔ anticodon UAC.
  • Protein shape → function. The amino acid order decides how the protein folds. Change the order and the shape (and the job) can change.

2. Kinds of mutations

TypeWhat happensEffect on protein
Silent (substitution)One base swapped, codon still means the same amino acid. GCA→GCG (Ala→Ala)None
Missense (substitution)One base swapped → a different amino acid. GAG→GUG (Glu→Val) = sickle cellNone to severe. Depends where and how different the new amino acid is
Nonsense (substitution)One base swapped → a STOP codon. UAC→UAA (Tyr→STOP)Protein cut short, usually doesn't work
Frameshift (insertion or deletion of 1 or 2 bases)Reading frame shifts, every codon after it changesUsually severe: many wrong amino acids, often an early STOP
Insertion/deletion of 3 basesAdds or removes one whole codon; frame staysOne amino acid added or missing; often milder
Frameshift trick: THE FAT CAT ATE THE RAT → delete the first F → THE ATC ATA TET HER AT. Everything after the error is garbage.

Other must-knows

  • Causes: random replication errors, or mutagens such as UV light (makes thymine dimers), X-rays, chemicals in tobacco smoke.
  • Body cell vs. sex cell: a mutation in a skin cell affects only that person (could lead to cancer). Only mutations in gametes (egg/sperm) pass to offspring.
  • Not always bad: most are neutral, some harmful, a few helpful. Mutations are the source of new variation for evolution.
  • Chromosome-level mutations (bigger): deletion, duplication, inversion, translocation of a chunk of chromosome.

4. Explain it (test-style short answer)

Q1. A DNA replication error changes one base. Explain two different ways this could affect the protein.

Check your answer has: (1) it could be silent: same amino acid because several codons code for the same one, so no change; (2) missense: a different amino acid → protein may fold differently → may not work (e.g., sickle cell hemoglobin); or nonsense: early STOP → short, non-working protein. Use the words codon, amino acid, shape, function.

Q2. Why is a one-base deletion usually worse than a one-base substitution?

Check your answer has: the deletion shifts the reading frame, so every codon after it is read wrong (many amino acids change, often an early STOP). A substitution changes at most one codon.

Q3. Claim-Evidence-Reasoning: "Sickle cell disease is caused by a mutation."

Check your answer has: Claim: one base substitution in the hemoglobin gene causes it. Evidence: the codon GAG becomes GUG, so glutamic acid (Glu) is replaced by valine (Val). Reasoning: the different amino acid changes how hemoglobin folds and sticks together → red blood cells become sickle-shaped → carry oxygen poorly and block vessels.
